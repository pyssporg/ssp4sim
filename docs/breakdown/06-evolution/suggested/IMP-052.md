# Executors take ownership of invocables

**Question:** Should executors take unique ownership of their invocables, replacing the current shared ownership?

**Evidence:** A raw developer note proposed propagating the top-layer refactor downward. The stepdata/resolver split is delivered: `StepData` carries timing only (`invocable.hpp:14-28`) and read policy lives in the resolver. Ownership is deliberately shared — executors take `std::vector<std::shared_ptr<Invocable>>`, with the rationale that executors may wrap each other and the graph may outlive a single executor (`executor_base.hpp:18-21`).

**Change:** Re-justify unique ownership against the documented shared-ownership rationale, or withdraw the ownership half; update the design docs in the same change.

**Risk:** Medium — ownership semantics ripple through all executor variants; the shared-ownership decision must be overturned explicitly, not silently.

**Acceptance:** A recorded ownership decision (adopt unique ownership and remove the shared-ownership rationale, or withdraw it); stepdata/resolver responsibilities stay as delivered; the full suite passes.
